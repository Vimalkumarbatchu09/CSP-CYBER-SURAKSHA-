Reference Glossary

Cybersecurity Terms You Should Know

A zero-day vulnerability is an undisclosed computer software flaw that hackers discover before the developers are aware of it. The term "zero-day" refers to the fact that developers have had zero days to patch it.

A VPN encrypts your internet traffic through an authenticated tunnel and routes it through a remote server, masking your IP address and safeguarding your private data from snoopers on public Wi-Fi.

An automated cyberattack where botnets test large databases of leaked username/password combinations across thousands of high-value sites (banking, social, shopping) to breach accounts that reuse passwords.

A secure communications protocol that prevents third parties (including internet service providers and telecom operators) from accessing plaintext data transferred from one endpoint to another.
Password Safety Handbook

Traditional password advice recommended frequent 30-day changes and arbitrary numbers. Modern NIST standards recommend:

  • Length is King: Use 14-16+ characters (passphrases like CorrectHorseBatteryStaple!).
  • Password Managers: Rely on vaults like Bitwarden, KeePass, or 1Password.
  • Breach Auditing: Check whether your email has leaked using trusted breach aggregators.
Phishing Defense Handbook

Phishing attacks succeed by evoking panic or greed. Remember the S.L.A.M. technique:

  • S - Sender: Inspect the actual email address, not just the display name.
  • L - Links: Hover to see destination URLs before clicking.
  • A - Attachments: Do not open unexpected .zip, .html, or .exe files.
  • M - Message: Be alert to artificial urgency or pressure to bypass standard verification.
MFA Implementation Guide

Rankings of MFA mechanisms from strongest to basic:

  1. FIDO2 / WebAuthn Hardware Keys: Phishing-resistant physical USB/NFC devices.
  2. Time-based OTP Apps (TOTP): Google Authenticator, Aegis, Bitwarden.
  3. Push Notifications: Mobile app approval prompts with number matching.
  4. SMS / Email OTP: Better than nothing, but susceptible to SIM-swapping.
Privacy Protection Handbook

Take charge of your personal digital footprint:

  • Disable location access for apps that do not require navigation.
  • Revoke advertising IDs on iOS and Android devices.
  • Review and revoke inactive OAuth permissions ("Login with Google/Facebook").
Safe Web Browsing Protocol

Protect against drive-by exploits and deceptive ads:

  • Install reputable ad-blockers like uBlock Origin to eliminate malvertising.
  • Configure DNS-over-HTTPS (DoH) via Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) or Quad9 (9.9.9.9).
  • Keep your browser updated to patch JavaScript engine zero-days.
Social Media Hardening

Avoid social engineering exploitation:

  • Never publish real-time vacation check-ins until you have returned home.
  • Keep accounts private; do not accept follower requests from strangers.
  • Beware of clone accounts mimicking friends asking for urgent loans.
Mobile & UPI Security Guide

Rules for mobile devices and UPI / online payments:

  • Remember: UPI PIN is entered only when SENDING money, never when receiving.
  • Do not install remote desktop apps (AnyDesk, TeamViewer) at the request of an unknown caller.
  • Set up SIM-lock PIN with your telecom carrier to prevent SIM swapping.
Cybersecurity Fundamentals

The core triad of cybersecurity:

  • Confidentiality: Only authorized individuals can view the data.
  • Integrity: Data remains accurate and unaltered during transit and storage.
  • Availability: Authorized users have reliable access to systems when needed.

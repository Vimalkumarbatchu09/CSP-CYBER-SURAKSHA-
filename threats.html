THREAT INTELLIGENCE & PREVENTION

Know the Threats Before They Strike

Understanding modern attack vectors is the first line of defense. Explore the 8 most prevalent cybersecurity threats targeting individuals and organizations, complete with warning signs and prevention steps.

Critical

Phishing

Fraudulent emails, fake websites, or SMS messages designed to trick you into revealing sensitive credentials, passwords, or credit card numbers.

High

Malware

Malicious software such as trojans, spyware, keyloggers, and viruses that infiltrate your device to steal data, monitor keystrokes, or corrupt files.

Critical

Ransomware

A catastrophic form of malware that rapidly encrypts your personal photos, documents, and hard drive, demanding cryptocurrency payment to unlock.

High

Password Attacks

Brute-force scripts, credential stuffing, and dictionary attacks that crack weak, predictable, or reused passwords across different websites.

Critical

Identity Theft

Criminals unlawfully acquire personal details (Aadhaar, SSN, PAN, date of birth) to open unauthorized loans, credit cards, or impersonate you.

High

Social Engineering

Psychological manipulation tactics that trick people into voluntarily handing over confidential security information or bypassing protocol.

Medium

Online Scams

Lottery fraud, fake job offers, romance scams, and bogus QR code payment requests that deceive victims into sending money directly.

High

Data Breaches

Security incidents where corporate databases are compromised by cyber adversaries, leaking millions of user emails, passwords, and records onto the dark web.

Threat Profile: Phishing Attacks
Severity: Critical
How It Works:

Phishing attacks use deceptive emails, spoofed sender addresses, and lookalike domain names (e.g. secure-paypa1.com) to impersonate banks, e-commerce giants, or IT departments. They inject an artificial sense of urgency (e.g., "Account suspended within 2 hours!") to compel victims to click links and type credentials.

Warning Signs:
  • Generic greetings ("Dear Customer" instead of your name).
  • Grammatical errors, odd phrasing, or mismatched domain extensions (.xyz, .top).
  • Demands for OTPs, PINs, or password resets that you did not initiate.
Defense Strategy:

Never click links in unexpected emails. Always open a fresh tab and navigate directly to the verified official portal. Use email providers with SPF/DKIM filters and enable MFA.

Threat Profile: Malware & Spyware
Severity: High
How It Works:

Malware (Malicious Software) is an umbrella term encompassing Trojans, Keyloggers, Adware, and Worms. It is typically distributed through pirated software torrents, infected email attachments (.exe, .scr, macro-enabled .doc), or drive-by downloads on compromised web pages.

Warning Signs:
  • Sudden sluggish performance and unusually high CPU or RAM consumption.
  • Spontaneous pop-ups, modified browser default search engine, or new unwanted toolbars.
  • Antivirus disabled without your consent.
Defense Strategy:

Never download cracked software or run unknown executables. Keep your operating system patched, and run a reputable antivirus solution with real-time heuristic scanning.

Threat Profile: Ransomware Extortion
Severity: Critical
How It Works:

Ransomware silently searches your storage drives for documents, databases, and photos, then encrypts them with military-grade algorithms (like AES-256 or RSA-4096). A ransom note replaces your desktop wallpaper demanding Bitcoin to provide the decryption key.

Key Risk:

Paying the ransom does NOT guarantee recovery and funds organized cybercrime.

Defense Strategy:

Follow the 3-2-1 Backup Rule: Maintain 3 copies of important data on 2 different media types, with 1 copy stored offline or off-site (isolated from network contagion).

Threat Profile: Password & Credential Attacks
Severity: High
How It Works:

Attackers utilize automated tools like Hashcat to test billions of dictionary combinations per second. In Credential Stuffing, attackers feed lists of leaked email/password pairs from third-party breaches into popular websites to see where victims reused credentials.

Defense Strategy:
  • Generate unique passwords with 14+ random characters.
  • Use a reputable Password Manager (e.g. Bitwarden, 1Password).
  • Always enforce Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).

Threat Profile: Identity Theft
Severity: Critical

Identity theft occurs when malicious actors harvest personally identifiable information (PII) to impersonate you. This can ruin credit scores, generate fraudulent tax claims, or result in criminal investigations under your name.

Prevention:
  • Never publicly post ID card photos, boarding passes, or birth certificates on social media.
  • Shred physical bank statements and official documents before discarding.
  • Monitor credit reports and active SIM cards registered under your national identity.
Threat Profile: Social Engineering
Severity: High

Social engineering bypasses firewalls by hacking the human mind. Attackers leverage authority ("This is the Police / CEO"), urgency ("Transfer money immediately"), or fear ("Your account will be terminated") to extract compliance.

Golden Rule:

Pause, take a breath, and verify through an independent communication channel whenever you encounter urgent demands for sensitive data or money transfers.

Threat Profile: Online Financial Scams
Severity: Medium to High

Online scams include fake e-commerce shopping storefronts, high-yield cryptocurrency investment schemes, work-from-home task frauds, and QR-code payment reversals where scanning actually debits your balance.

Remember:

Scanning a UPI / Payment QR code is only used to pay money, never to receive money. If an offer looks too good to be true, it is almost certainly a scam.

Threat Profile: Enterprise Data Breaches
Severity: High

Even if your personal computer is secure, companies where you have registered accounts may suffer database breaches. Stolen databases containing hashes, names, and phone numbers are sold on underground cyber forums.

Response:
  • Check if your email has surfaced in breaches using services like HaveIBeenPwned.
  • Immediately reset passwords for any affected service.
  • Never reuse your main email password on third-party forums.

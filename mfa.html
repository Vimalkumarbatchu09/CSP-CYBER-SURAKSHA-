ZERO TRUST AUTHENTICATION

Protect Your Accounts with Multi-Factor Authentication

Multi-Factor Authentication adds an additional layer of security by requiring more than one method of verification. Even if an adversary steals your password, MFA stops them dead in their tracks.

The 3 Factors of Identity

The Building Blocks of Verification

True MFA combines at least two distinct authentication factors across separate categories.

Factor 1: Knowledge

Password

"Something You Know"

The traditional first line of defense. A secret sequence of characters or PIN known exclusively by the user.

Examples: Complex Passwords, PINs, Passphrases, Security Question Answers.
Factor 2: Possession

OTP / Verification Code

"Something You Have"

A physical or cryptographic item in your possession that receives or generates time-sensitive one-time passcodes.

Examples: Authenticator Apps (TOTP), SMS OTP, Hardware FIDO Keys (YubiKey), Push Prompts.
Factor 3: Inherence

Biometric Authentication

"Something You Are"

Unique biological or behavioral traits that cannot be easily stolen, replicated, or guessed by a remote hacker.

Examples: Fingerprint Scan (TouchID), Facial Recognition (FaceID), Retina / Voice Scans.

Practical Implementation

How to Enable MFA on Common Platforms

Google Account
  1. Go to myaccount.google.com.
  2. Click on Security on the left.
  3. Select 2-Step Verification.
  4. Choose Google Authenticator or Security Key.
  5. Save offline backup codes safely.
Microsoft Account
  1. Navigate to account.microsoft.com/security.
  2. Choose Advanced Security Options.
  3. Turn on Two-Step Verification.
  4. Link Microsoft Authenticator app.
  5. Verify via push notification.
Social Media (IG/X)
  1. Open app Settings & Privacy.
  2. Tap Accounts Center > Password & Security.
  3. Tap Two-Factor Authentication.
  4. Select Authenticator App (recommended over SMS).
  5. Download backup recovery codes.
Why Multi-Factor Authentication is Indispensable
The Password Vulnerability Paradox:

Passwords are prone to human memory limits. As a result, users frequently choose predictable passwords, reuse them across multiple services, or fall victim to phishing emails and data breaches.

How MFA Solves This:

By mandating two or more authentication methods from distinct categories, an attacker with a compromised password still cannot penetrate your account without simultaneously possessing your physical token or biometric credential. According to Microsoft & Google security studies, enabling MFA blocks over 99.9% of automated account takeover attacks.

Best Practice Tip:

Opt for App-based TOTP (like Google Authenticator, Bitwarden, or Aegis) or Hardware FIDO Keys over SMS codes, as SMS messages can occasionally be intercepted via SIM-swapping attacks.

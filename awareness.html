CYBER HYGIENE & EDUCATION

Essential Cyber Safety Tips

Developing disciplined digital instincts is the most effective safeguard against cyberattacks. Review our core safety tenets below to fortify your everyday online presence.

Defense in Depth

8 Golden Rules of Cyber Defense

Think Before Clicking Links

Hover over hyperlinks before clicking to preview the real URL. Watch out for misspelled brand names, urgency triggers, and shortened redirect URLs.

Never Share OTPs

No genuine bank official, support agent, or police representative will ever ask for your One-Time Password. Sharing your OTP gives attackers direct account access.

Use Unique Passwords

Never duplicate passwords across multiple platforms. If one site suffers a breach, password reuse allows attackers to breach all your accounts via credential stuffing.

Enable MFA

Turn on Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) across your email, social networks, and banking portals. It blocks 99.9% of automated credential takeover attempts.

Keep Software Updated

Software updates frequently contain critical security patches that fix vulnerabilities exploited by malware and ransomware. Enable automatic OS and app updates.

Avoid Unknown Downloads

Never download cracked games, pirated software, or email attachments ending in .exe, .scr, or .vbs from unverified sources, as they often contain Trojans.

Check Website URLs Carefully

Look for the HTTPS padlock indicator and verify the spelling of the domain. Fake sites often use typos like netfl1x.com or gooogle.co.

Do Not Share Sensitive Info Online

Avoid posting boarding passes, IDs, home addresses, phone numbers, or credit card snapshots on public social media where fraudsters can collect personal data.

Quick Reference Guide

Cyber Hygiene: Do’s & Don'ts

A scannable guide comparing risky habits against recommended security actions.

Recommended (DO)

  • Use a Password Manager: Store long, randomly generated passwords securely so you never have to remember dozens of complex phrases.
  • Verify Senders Independently: Call your bank or contact the vendor using phone numbers from their official website if you receive urgent payment alerts.
  • Backup to Offline Storage: Keep an external hard drive disconnected from the internet to preserve clean copies of your files.
  • Use a VPN on Public Networks: Always encrypt your web browsing traffic when connected to airports, coffee shops, or hotels.

Risky Practices (DON'T)

  • Don't Scan Random QR Codes: Scanning a QR code from an unverified caller or flyer can instantly authorize an outgoing digital money transfer.
  • Don't Save Passwords in Unprotected Notes: Writing PINs and master credentials in plain text notes or emails leaves them exposed.
  • Don't Install Pirated / Torrents: Cracked Adobe or Windows installers are the #1 delivery vector for cryptocurrency miners and info-stealers.
  • Don't Ignore Security Alerts: When your browser displays a red warning certificate alert, immediately exit instead of clicking "Proceed Anyway".
